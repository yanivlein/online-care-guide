How We Rate

Choosing an online healthcare service involves more than a price or a popular brand name. ConsumerMed organizes comparisons around factors that readers can investigate before deciding whether to speak with a provider. The relative importance of these factors can differ by category, and a strong comparison result does not establish that a service is clinically right for you.

Our Core Comparison Criteria

Clinical Approach and Safety Information

We consider whether the service describes its clinical evaluation process, eligibility requirements, medication or treatment options, screening, follow-up, and safety-related information. We distinguish between FDA-approved medications and compounded medications when that distinction is relevant.

Access to Licensed Clinicians

We compare how patients may reach licensed healthcare professionals, including consultation formats, follow-up visits, messaging, and available clinician support, as described by the provider.

Price Transparency and Coverage

We look for clarity about consultation or membership fees, medication costs, additional charges, cancellation terms, insurance, and HSA/FSA information where applicable. A displayed starting price may not represent a patient’s full cost.

Convenience and Service Experience

We consider online onboarding, communication, prescription fulfillment or pharmacy arrangements where relevant, shipping or pickup options, and the information available about account management and support.

Information Quality and Customer Support

We consider how clearly a provider explains its services, limitations, eligibility, and support. We may note publicly available feedback when it can be contextualized, but we do not treat unverified testimonials as clinical evidence.

Category-Specific Factors

Weight Management & GLP-1

Relevant factors may include medication access, prescription screening, dose-adjustment and side-effect support, nutrition or lifestyle coaching, pricing by treatment option, and insurance assistance. A program’s availability of a medication does not guarantee that a person will qualify to receive it.

Menopause & HRT

Relevant factors may include the clinician’s approach to perimenopause or menopause care, available hormonal and nonhormonal options, follow-up, treatment forms, risk screening, and cost clarity. We distinguish compounded therapies from FDA-approved products where applicable.

Women’s Sexual Health and Libido

Relevant factors may include the stated treatment indication, whether a treatment is hormonal or nonhormonal, daily or on-demand use, clinician assessment, eligibility, and the evidence and safety information available for the specific product. Options on a single comparison page may serve different symptoms or patient populations.

How Rankings and Scores Should Be Read

Our lists and labels are comparative editorial tools, not clinical diagnoses or guarantees. Inclusion, order, and the number of providers shown may differ by category and commercial availability. Compensation can influence placement; see our Disclosure for details.

Sources and Updates

Provider websites, official terms and pricing pages, regulatory information, and recognized clinical references may inform our editorial work. When details are unclear, they should be qualified or left out rather than estimated. We aim to review material changes, but provider details can change before a page is updated.

We do not claim to assess every provider or verify an individual patient’s outcome. Always confirm current details with the provider and discuss treatment decisions with your clinician.

Advertising and Editorial Context

Some partners compensate ConsumerMed for qualifying referrals. These arrangements may affect which brands appear and where they are displayed. We disclose material relationships, and a paid listing does not mean the provider is best for every reader. Our Disclosure explains this in more detail.
